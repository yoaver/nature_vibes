# Animal silhouette comparison

Reviewed 8 November 2026 against reference photographs, enlarged native models, and day/night scene captures. This is a visual assessment of the existing stylized artwork, with proposed changes ordered by their effect on recognition. Photos are references, not replacement scene assets.

## Recommended order

1. Distinguish the two macaws through bill/head proportions, especially the hyacinth's heavy bill.
2. Reduce the rabbit/mouse impression in the Seba's bat model.
3. Refine squirrel-monkey head, torso, and tail proportions across walking, hanging, and climbing poses together.
4. Refine caiman snout depth and tail/body balance.
5. Make restrained capybara head/back adjustments, preserving their relaxed expression.
6. Transfer the sea lions' improved walking proportions consistently into their resting and swimming models.

## Jungle

| Animal | Existing silhouette compared with photos | Proposed adjustment | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult capybara | Recognizable barrel body, small ears, and no visible tail. The rump is rather round and the legs read as narrow sticks beneath it. Some grazing angles make the muzzle look too tapered. | Slightly flatten the back, give the shoulder-to-neck transition more continuity, and preserve a deep, blunt muzzle through head rotations. Broaden the feet subtly rather than enlarging the legs. | Medium |
| Baby capybara | Larger head relative to body reads appropriately as a youngster. Existing blunt face works better than some adult grazing views. | Preserve the head/body balance. Apply the adult foot and muzzle refinements lightly, without giving babies adult proportions. | Low |
| Spectacled caiman, bank | Low body and raised eye ridge read correctly. The alert snout becomes a thin projecting bar; the tail contributes relatively little to the overall silhouette. | Give the snout a deeper tapered wedge and clearer transition into the skull. Increase the tail's visual share with a gradual taper, preserving the low body and splayed feet. | Medium–high |
| Spectacled caiman, swimming | Only the upper head, back, and tail should dominate this view. Existing clipping largely supports that reading. | Carry over the revised snout and tail. Keep most of the torso submerged; do not enlarge the swimming animal again. | Medium |
| Squirrel monkey, walking | Slender limbs work, but the head is large relative to the short, oval torso. The curling tail looks shorter than its balance-tail role suggests. | Reduce the round skull slightly, lengthen/slim the torso, and give the tail a longer, more descending sweep. Keep the rear haunch raised and hands small. | High |
| Squirrel monkey, hanging/climbing/sleeping | Hanging is charming and recognizable. Tail hooks and round heads make the species less specific. Curled sleeping clusters work at scene scale. | Apply shared head/tail proportions to every active pose. Preserve the four-limb separation and existing branch contacts. Keep sleeping silhouettes compact and irregular. | Medium |
| Scarlet macaw | Long tail and upright perch are good. The small bill and round head read as a generic parrot; the upper bill's pale mass is missing from the profile. | Broaden the hooked upper bill, strengthen the forehead/bill join, and taper the long tail more clearly. Keep the muted scene colors and current small overall size. | High |
| Hyacinth macaw | Shape is almost identical to the scarlet model despite the reference's markedly heavier bill. The yellow facial patch can read like the scarlet's pale cheek. | Use a larger, deeper black bill and fuller head/neck. Keep yellow concentrated around the eye and lower bill base. Recognition should survive recoloring the bird black. | High |
| Toco toucan | Long bill, distinct neck, and tail now produce one of the clearer species silhouettes. Upright and leaning poses both read well. | Preserve the accepted overall proportions. At most smooth the wing-to-back join and foot contact in the horizontal pose. No renewed bill/head overhaul is warranted. | Low |
| Three-banded armadillo | Dome, three bands, pointed head, and short limbs read immediately. The model's head projects horizontally and the shell can look like a perfect helmet; the photograph shows a more tucked, downward-facing head. | Angle the muzzle down slightly, soften the dome's front/rear asymmetry, and give the tail a thicker base and shorter taper. Preserve the distinctive shuffling gait. | Low–medium |
| Seba's short-tailed bat | The large upright ears, angular projecting muzzle, and large head produce a rabbit/mouse impression. The wing silhouette is recognizable but simplified. | Make the head smaller and muzzle shorter/blunter; use broad rounded ears and a small nose leaf. Keep the compact body and short tail membrane. Retain the small, dark on-screen size and numerous colony members. | High |
| Morpho butterflies | Blue flashes read well at their tiny scene size. Enlarged wings are rather narrow and heart-like, with very heavy borders. | Broaden the forewings, round the hindwings, and reduce the relative outline thickness. A brown folded underside would improve landed views. Keep their current modest size and depth distribution. | Low–medium |
| Fireflies | They are rendered as points of light, not visible insect bodies. | Keep them abstract. Anatomical silhouettes would add visual clutter at this distance. | No change |

## Bay

| Animal | Existing silhouette compared with photos | Proposed adjustment | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| California sea lion, walking | Recent fuller chest/abdomen and broader neck bring the silhouette much closer to the reference. Paired flipper planting and rear-led movement are important to preserve. | Polish the chest-to-neck taper across all four gaits. Maintain short planted strides and avoid exposing the internal torso sections as separate bumps. | Low–medium |
| California sea lion, resting/sitting/swimming | These use a different body drawing from the walking model, so the same individual can become noticeably slimmer when its activity changes. Adult-male mass is strongest in the upright pose. | Share a consistent chest/neck envelope across activities. Preserve one large adult male, slimmer junior males, and rounder-headed pups. A resting adult male should retain more forequarter mass than a female. | Medium |
| Bottlenose dolphin | Melon-to-beak transition, swept dorsal fin, and tapering body identify a dolphin. The offshore pod's scale works. Tail/flipper detail becomes weak in the small jumping silhouette. | Refine the rounded forehead and short rostrum, with a clearer tail-peduncle taper and fluke notch. Preserve the fixed takeoff direction, nose-first entry, and quiet offshore placement. | Low–medium |
| Distant seabirds | Simple gull-like arcs function as distant birds. At this size they are symbols rather than species portraits. | Keep the broad wing silhouette and occasional slight elbow bend. Do not add enlarged heads, feet, or detailed plumage. A specific gull species need not be asserted. | No substantial change |
